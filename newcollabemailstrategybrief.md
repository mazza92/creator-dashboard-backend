# Newcollab Email System: Full Strategy Overhaul

## Executive Summary

The current email system is 7 transactional nudges that read as system notifications. Users bounce after 1-2 unlocks because we're pushing them to "pitch more" without teaching them WHY brands say no, HOW to fix it, or WHAT successful creators actually do. The tone is robotic, the content is thin, and there's no consistent voice.

This brief replaces the current system with an **educational, story-driven, manager-voiced email strategy** built around three user segments (Explorer / Doubter / Maximizer) and a weekly rhythm that keeps creators engaged even between actions.

Target: reduce 7-day post-signup drop-off by 40%, lift Explorer → Doubter progression by 60%, lift Doubter → Maximizer → Pro by 3× over 90 days.

---

## Part 1 — Diagnosis: what's actually broken

### The 3 systemic failures

**1. No educational spine.**
Every current email pushes the user to *do something* (pitch, follow up, update pipeline). Zero emails *teach* something. Users signing up don't understand PR mechanics, so pushing them to pitch harder is like pushing a beginner cook to make more meals without teaching them how to hold a knife.

**2. Wrong voice.**
Emails read as system-generated ("Time to follow up"). But your product IS the AI Manager. Every email should come *from the manager* — a warm coach, not a cron job. The disconnect between in-app voice and email voice erodes brand trust.

**3. Segment-blind delivery.**
Every user gets the same 7-email drip regardless of state:
- Explorers (1 unlock) get pushed to pitch more
- Doubters (2 unlocks, 0 replies) get pushed to pitch more
- Maximizers (3/3 used) get pushed to pitch more

Same message, three different states, three different needs. All three feel unheard.

### What the Creator Base data shows

Looking at your 1,628 creators:
- **~65% are recent signups** (1-3 weeks old, low unlock activity)
- **~85% are on Free tier** (with only ~1% converted to Pro visible in the sample)
- **~40% have unlocked 1-2 times** but never a 3rd — they're stuck in the "doubter" zone
- **~30% never unlocked at all** — the explorer wall is even earlier

The pattern: users try 1-2 unlocks, hit no replies, doubt whether it works, and go silent. Your emails don't address that emotional state. They just say "pitch another brand."

## Part 2 — The new segmentation framework

Every email lives inside one of these user states. State determines content, tone, and timing.

| State | Definition | Emotional need | Content type |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New** | Just signed up, 0 unlocks, Day 0-3 | Confidence, orientation | Welcome + manager intro |
| **Explorer** | 1 unlock, no reply yet | Education, encouragement | "How PR actually works" series |
| **Engaged** | 2 unlocks, waiting on replies | Patience, tactical advice | "What to do while waiting" |
| **Doubter** | 2+ unlocks, 14+ days, no replies | Reframe, hope, tactic pivot | Similar-creator success stories |
| **Maximizer** | 3/3 used, engaged, some replies | Momentum, Pro upgrade path | Pro coaching value |
| **Winner** | Received first reply / PR box | Celebration + expansion | Growth beyond first win |
| **Dormant** | 14+ days no login, any state | Re-entry, new value hook | New brands + fresh insight |

Each state gets its own email content library, its own send cadence, and its own conversion goal.

## Part 3 — The educational spine (the biggest missing piece)

Newcollab needs an *ongoing content series* that teaches creators the PR game, so paying $19/mo feels like tuition to a masterclass rather than paying for a directory.

### The 6-part "Creator PR Fundamentals" series

Delivered to Explorers over Days 4-20. One per 2-3 days. Every email teaches ONE concept:

1. **"Why brands say no (it's not what you think)"** — the 5 real reasons pitches fail (aesthetic, contact, recency, follower-inflation, generic pitches)
2. **"The 60-second brand audit"** — how PR coordinators actually review your profile
3. **"The pitch structure that gets replied to"** — anatomy of a working cold pitch
4. **"Follow-up is where deals actually happen"** — the 3-touch cadence that lifts reply rate 4×
5. **"How micro-creators land deals macros can't"** — the specific edge you have
6. **"Building your rate card without a rate"** — how to charge for UGC when you're new

Each email:
- Under 250 words
- One concrete tactic
- One creator example
- One CTA to apply the tactic in-app

**Why this works:** creators start associating Newcollab with *learning how to become the creator brands reply to*. That's the north-star positioning we discussed. Every educational email reinforces the coach relationship.

### The weekly Manager Digest (Mondays)

Send every Monday at 8am local time. Consistent, reliable rhythm.

Contents rotate weekly but always include:
- **This week's focus** — one tactical theme (e.g., "Follow-ups")
- **New brands added this week** — 3-5 hand-picked, briefly explained
- **Creator win of the week** — real anonymized story
- **Your manager's tip** — one 2-3 sentence tactical insight
- **Your progress since last week** — score change, unlocks used, replies received (if any)

**Purpose:** a reliable weekly touchpoint that isn't a nag. Users start expecting it. Even users who don't open every one see the sender name and feel the ongoing relationship.

## Part 7 — Trigger rules + throttling

### The new global rules

- **Max 2 emails per week per user** (up from 1/day, 3/week — allows the education series to actually flow)
- **Onboarding sequence** exempt from throttling (Days 0-3 always deliver)
- **Weekly digest** always Mondays — treated as the anchor email
- **Behavioral triggers** deliver on top of digest, but only 1 per week max
- **Silent hours:** never send between 10pm-6am user local time

### Priority when conflicts arise

If multiple emails could trigger the same week:
1. Onboarding (unmovable)
2. Behavioral trigger (state changed)
3. Weekly digest
4. Educational series

Skip lower-priority if user is at their weekly cap.

### Segment-based trigger rules

```
Explorer (1 unlock, no reply)
  → Educational series (Days 5, 8, 12, 16, 20)
  → Weekly digest (Mondays)
  → Behavioral triggers as they happen

Doubter (2 unlocks, 14+ days no reply)
  → Pause educational series
  → Send Email 14 (story) immediately
  → Send Email 15 (5-pitch reframe) 4 days later
  → Resume weekly digest

Maximizer (3/3 unlocks used)
  → Send Email 16 (quota resets in N days) immediately
  → Send Email 17 (What Pro creators do) 2 days later
  → Send Email 18 (Manager's Pro plan) 7 days later
  → Weekly digest continues

Dormant (14+ days no login)
  → Send Email 19 (new brands you missed) at day 14
  → Send Email 20 (Manager wants you back) at day 21
  → No further emails until they log in
  → Reset segment on login
```

## Part 8 — Voice + tone guide

Every email, every subject line, every preheader must follow these rules.

### Do

- Speak first-person as the manager ("I", "your manager")
- Warm, direct, peer-to-peer
- One idea per email
- Specific numbers and real examples
- Lowercase subject lines
- Contractions ("I'm", "you've")
- One clear CTA

### Never

- Em dashes
- "AI" or "the system" (always "your manager")
- Exclamation marks in subject lines
- All caps for emphasis
- Emoji in body copy (occasional in subject lines/preheaders okay)
- Corporate: "leverage", "elevate", "unleash", "solutions"
- AI-tell openers: "I came across", "I noticed your"
- Pressure language: "Don't miss out!", "Last chance!"
- Generic phrases: "hope this helps", "let me know"

### The one-sentence voice test

If a real human talent manager wouldn't send it to a real creator client, don't send it.

## Part 13 — Ongoing content ops

Emails aren't ship-and-forget. Assign a weekly ritual:

- **Every Monday:** Someone writes the week's Manager Digest theme
- **Every Friday:** Review last week's email performance (open, click, unsubscribe by segment)
- **Every month:** Cull the lowest-performing email in each series and rewrite
- **Every quarter:** Fresh set of educational topics for the series

Email content is a living asset. Budget ~2 hours/week ongoing.
